Pentagon designates Anthropic as supply chain risk; first US AI firm to receive classification

The Department of Defense has designated Anthropic, the AI safety company founded by former OpenAI executives, as a supply chain risk under federal procurement regulations. The classification, confirmed March 6, marks the first time a US-based artificial intelligence company has received this designation, which typically restricts or prohibits federal agencies from purchasing the company's products or services. Anthropic announced it will challenge the designation in federal court, though the company has not disclosed the specific grounds for the Pentagon's decision.

The supply chain risk framework, established under Section 889 of the 2019 NDAA, allows defense officials to exclude vendors deemed to pose national security risks to federal information systems. Previous designations have primarily targeted Chinese telecommunications firms including Huawei and ZTE. The extension to a US AI company represents a significant expansion of the framework's application, particularly given Anthropic's positioning as a safety-focused alternative to other large language model developers.

Anthropic currently operates Claude, a large language model that competes with OpenAI's GPT series and Google's Gemini. The company has raised approximately $7.3 billion in funding, with major backing from Google and Amazon. Federal contracts represent a relatively small portion of Anthropic's current revenue base, though the designation could complicate the company's ability to participate in the growing federal AI procurement market.

Assessment

The Anthropic designation signals a potential shift in how defense officials view AI supply chain risks, moving beyond foreign adversary concerns to include domestic companies. This could reflect either specific security concerns about Anthropic's operations, partnerships, or data handling practices, or a broader tightening of AI vendor scrutiny as these systems become more integrated into defense applications. The timing coincides with increased congressional pressure on AI safety and security standards, suggesting this may not be an isolated action.
