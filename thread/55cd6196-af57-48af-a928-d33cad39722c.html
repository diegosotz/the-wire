Treasury grants India 30-day waiver to purchase Russian crude amid Iran conflict

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent issued a 30-day sanctions waiver allowing India to purchase Russian crude oil, describing the move as a "stop gap measure" during the ongoing Iran conflict. The waiver temporarily suspends secondary sanctions that would otherwise penalize Indian entities for transactions with Russian oil exporters. India has maintained significant Russian crude imports since 2022, with purchases averaging 1.6-1.8 million barrels per day through 2025.

The timing connects to broader U.S. energy security positioning as Iranian oil exports face potential disruption. Iran currently exports approximately 1.3 million barrels per day, primarily to China and India. The waiver ensures continued crude flows to India's refining system while the administration pursues domestic production expansion through DPA measures and infrastructure investment.

The decision represents a tactical shift from previous sanctions enforcement, where Treasury maintained pressure on Indian refiners to reduce Russian crude purchases. Current global oil prices have stabilized around $78-82 per barrel following initial Iran conflict volatility.

Assessment

The sanctions waiver signals the administration is prioritizing supply stability over sanctions enforcement during the Iran crisis. This temporary accommodation for India's Russian crude purchases reduces immediate supply pressure while domestic DPA measures and the Reliance refinery project address longer-term capacity. The 30-day timeframe suggests Treasury expects either Iran conflict resolution or alternative supply arrangements within that window. The policy coordination indicates energy security considerations are driving both the emergency domestic measures and the tactical sanctions flexibility.

Trump announces Reliance Industries to build first US refinery in 50 years

President Trump announced that India's Reliance Industries will invest in constructing the first new oil refinery in the United States in five decades. The announcement comes one day before the Defense Production Act designation for Sable Offshore, suggesting a coordinated push to expand domestic energy infrastructure capacity. No financial details, timeline, or location for the Reliance facility were disclosed in the initial announcement.

The refinery investment represents a significant shift in U.S. energy infrastructure development, which has focused primarily on upgrading existing facilities rather than greenfield construction since the 1970s. Current U.S. refining capacity stands at approximately 18.1 million barrels per day across 129 active refineries, with utilization rates averaging 85-90% in recent years.

Reliance Industries operates the world's largest refining complex at Jamnagar, India, with capacity of 1.24 million barrels per day. The company's technical expertise in complex refining processes and petrochemical integration could signal plans for a sophisticated facility rather than basic crude processing capacity.

Assessment

The timing suggests the administration is pursuing a broader domestic energy expansion strategy beyond upstream production. Announcing major refining investment immediately before invoking emergency powers for Sable Offshore indicates coordination between downstream capacity additions and upstream production acceleration. This combination addresses both crude production bottlenecks and refining capacity constraints that have historically limited U.S. energy independence during supply disruptions.

Trump invokes emergency law for California oil producer Sable

President Trump invoked emergency powers under the Defense Production Act to support California oil producer Sable Offshore Corp, according to Bloomberg News reporting March 12. The move grants federal authority to expedite permitting, secure supply chains, and potentially override state regulatory barriers for the company's operations. Sable operates several offshore platforms in federal waters off California's coast, producing approximately 45,000 barrels per day as of Q4 2025 data.

The emergency designation comes amid broader tensions between federal and California state authorities over energy policy. California has maintained strict environmental regulations on offshore drilling operations, while federal agencies have pushed for expanded domestic production. The DPA invocation bypasses normal interagency coordination requirements and grants Sable priority status for equipment procurement and regulatory approvals.

Sable's stock jumped 23% in after-hours trading following the Bloomberg report. WTI crude futures showed minimal immediate reaction, trading flat at $78.42 per barrel as of Thursday's close. The company has been seeking federal support for expansion of its Platform Charlie operations, which have faced permitting delays since late 2024.

Assessment

This represents an escalation in federal override of state energy policy, using national security justification for what appears to be a relatively small-scale operation. The DPA invocation for a single mid-tier producer suggests either Sable has strategic importance not reflected in its production numbers, or this is a test case for broader federal intervention in California energy policy. The muted crude price reaction indicates markets view this as political theater rather than material supply impact, but the precedent could matter more than the immediate production effects.
